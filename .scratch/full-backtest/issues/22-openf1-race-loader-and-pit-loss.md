# 22: OpenF1 race loader (race outcome, pit loss, historical degradation)

**What to build:** Finish `openf1_loader` by adding the race side:
- `_race_stints` from `/v1/stints?session_key=<race>`.
- `_neutralisation_periods` from `/v1/race_control`, with explicit end-event handling: VSC starts on "VIRTUAL SAFETY CAR DEPLOYED" and ends on "VIRTUAL SAFETY CAR ENDING" (same `lap_number`); SC starts on "SAFETY CAR DEPLOYED" and ends on "SAFETY CAR IN THIS LAP" (same `lap_number`). Red flag is not a neutralisation (matches current loader). If an "end" event is missing, cap the period at the race lap count and log a quality warning.
- `_finishing_status` from `/v1/session_result`: `took_flag = not dnf and not dns and not dsq`.
- `_pit_losses_in` and `_historical_stint_rates` using lap times from `/v1/laps` with the neutralisation ranges above as the green-flag filter. **Do not** use `/v1/pit.pit_duration` for Pit Loss — at São Paulo R 2024 the mean `pit_duration` was 661 s because the red-flag parking time was counted as a pit stop.

## Implementation notes

**What was built (2026-10-23), test-first.**

- `src/f1_strategy/openf1_loader.py` — extended with the race side. One
  shared `_load_race_rows(client, race_session_key)` fetches `/v1/laps`,
  `/v1/stints`, `/v1/pit`, `/v1/race_control` and `/v1/session_result`
  for the target race session; `load_weekend` wires those into the four
  race-side builders:
  - `_race_stints(stints_rows, tla_by_num, drivers)` groups
    `/v1/stints` rows into `Stint(driver, compound, start_lap, end_lap)`
    tuples, keyed on TLA (not driver number) so the output matches
    `fastf1_loader._race_stints`. Rows for drivers outside the starter
    set are dropped (DNS reserves). Output is sorted by `(driver,
    start_lap)` for determinism.
  - `_neutralisation_periods(race_control_rows, race_laps)` ports the
    event-reconstruction rules the findings pin: `VIRTUAL SAFETY CAR
    DEPLOYED`/`ENDING` opens-and-closes a VSC on the same lap number;
    `SAFETY CAR DEPLOYED` / `SAFETY CAR IN THIS LAP` opens an SC that
    ends on its `IN THIS LAP` message's `lap_number`. Red flag is not
    a neutralisation (matches `fastf1_loader._lap_neutralisation_kind`,
    ADR-0004). A missing end event caps the period at `race_laps` and
    returns a quality warning (also logged at WARNING on the module
    logger); the function returns `(periods, warnings)`.
  - `_finishing_status(result_rows, tla_by_num, drivers)` reads
    `took_flag = not dnf and not dns and not dsq` directly off
    `/v1/session_result`'s explicit booleans. Lapped finishers have
    `dnf=False` on OpenF1 and are counted as took-the-flag, matching
    the FastF1 loader's `"Lapped" or "+N Lap"` behaviour.
  - `_pit_losses_in(laps_rows, pit_rows, neutralised_laps)` ports
    `fastf1_loader._pit_losses_in` on top of OpenF1 rows. Per driver:
    reference = `statistics.median` of green `lap_duration` values
    (`lap_number > 1`, not pit-in, not pit-out via `is_pit_out_lap`,
    not in `neutralised_laps`, non-null duration), requires ≥ 5
    reference laps, then loss = `(in - ref) + (out - ref)` across the
    `/v1/pit` lap and its `lap_number + 1` out-lap, clipped to
    `10 < loss < 60`. Deliberately never reads `/v1/pit.pit_duration`
    (findings: red-flag parking counted as a pit stop, mean 661 s at
    São Paulo).
  - `_historical_stint_rates(laps_rows, stints_rows, pit_rows,
    neutralised_laps, fuel_effect_s_per_lap, min_stint_length)` is the
    port of the FastF1 helper of the same name: per dry-compound stint,
    drop pit-in / pit-out / neutralised / null-duration laps, keep the
    longest strictly-consecutive tyre-age sub-run, fit a least-squares
    slope and add the fuel effect. `_longest_consecutive` and `_slope`
    mirror the FastF1 shapes line-for-line so a cross-source history
    fits uniformly.
- `_past_races_at_circuit` is OpenF1-only in this ticket's scope. It
  walks `range(max(era_start(race_season), OPENF1_FIRST_SEASON),
  race_season)` and calls `load_past_race_samples(client, season,
  alias)` for each alias in `location_group(circuit)` (ticket 16's
  alias groups shared with the FastF1 loader). The same recent-seasons
  weight `0.5 ** (race_season - season - 1)` and lap-count × season-
  weight weighted mean as the FastF1 helper. An empty past-season
  range (a 2023 race) returns `(0.0, (), {})` — not an error, and the
  seam ticket 23 is expected to fill in from FastF1 for 2022.
- `load_past_race_samples(client, season, event, *,
  fuel_effect_s_per_lap, history_min_stint_length) -> PastRaceSamples`
  is the public cross-source seam ticket 23 should pair with a parallel
  FastF1 function (see "How ticket 23 uses this" below). Returns raw
  per-race samples (list of pit losses, list of (compound, rate, n)),
  not aggregated numbers.
- `tests/fixtures/openf1/monza_2024.json` — extended with race rows for
  session_key 9590 (laps, stints, pit, race_control, session_result)
  and a tiny Monza 2023 past-season weekend (meeting 1210, race session
  9333) so `load_weekend` has at least one past-OpenF1 race to measure

- `tests/fixtures/openf1/austria_2024.json` — race rows for session 9550

- `tests/fixtures/openf1/sao_paulo_2024.json` — new. Hand-built for the

- `tests/test_openf1_race_loader.py` — new, 25 fast tests (~0.5 s). Unit

**Decisions and alternatives rejected.**

- *`load_past_race_samples` returns raw per-race samples, not an
  aggregated number.* The ticket 23 merger needs to see each past
  season's samples separately so it can apply one shared weighting
  across FastF1 (pre-2023) and OpenF1 (2023+) samples. Returning a
  pre-aggregated per-source number would force ticket 23 to re-derive
  weights, so the seam is the raw list. The internal
  `_past_races_at_circuit` applies the same weighting the FastF1
  loader already uses, so the OpenF1-only path works end-to-end today.
- *Keep `neutralised_laps` as a plain `set[int]` on the public seam.*
  `_pit_losses_in` and `_historical_stint_rates` both need O(1) lookup
  per lap. A caller gets the set from
  `_neutralised_lap_set(periods)`; a non-neutralised past race just
  passes `set()`.
- *Return quality warnings from `_neutralisation_periods` as a tuple
  rather than raising.* A missing SC/VSC "end" event isn't a reason to
  reject the whole race — the cap-at-race-lap-count rule the ticket
  calls out produces a usable period and a human-readable note. The
  warning is also logged at WARNING level on `openf1_loader._LOGGER` so
  it surfaces in Backtest output.
- *`took_flag = not (dnf or dns or dsq)` straight from the session
  result.* OpenF1's three explicit booleans leave no ambiguity, unlike
  FastF1's `Status` string. No special-case needed for lapped
  finishers (OpenF1's `dnf` is False for them).
- *Clip Pit Loss to `10 < loss < 60`, exactly as the FastF1 helper.*
  The findings don't argue for a different band and keeping identical
  clips means a cross-source history's distribution isn't skewed by
  one source letting outliers through.
- *Fetch `race_control` for a past race even when green.* The scan is
  one HTTP call cached on disk; the alternative — only fetching it if
  a later step needs it — complicates the row-set builder and saves
  roughly one request per past-circuit visit. Not worth the complexity
  on a 30-req/min budget. For the three sampled weekends a season's
  past-race scan is ≤ 6 requests × 1 past race = 6 requests.
- *Non-dry compounds (INTERMEDIATE / WET) are kept in `_race_stints`
  but excluded from `_historical_stint_rates`.* Mirrors the FastF1
  loader. The Dry Race filter in `predict()` reads the stint list and
  rejects a wet race on it; Historical Degradation is a dry-only
  concept per CONTEXT.md and ADR-0003.
- *Fast test uses a hand-built 2023 Monza past-season fixture, not a
  recorded one from the real cache.* The real `.cache/openf1/` only
  carries the ticket-21 pre-race session for Monza 2024. Recording a
  real 2023 Monza race would bloat the fixture (hundreds of lap rows)
  and tie the test to one real sample. The hand-built fixture pins
  the derivation; the slow tests pin the real-weekend path.

**Two bugs found and fixed while running the slow tests (2026-10-26).**

- *OpenF1 returns 404 with `{"detail":"No results found."}` for
  endpoint+filter combinations that have zero rows.* First saw it on
  `/v1/pit?session_key=9635` (São Paulo 2024 Sprint — no pit stops).
  The ticket-20 client previously treated every 4xx-other-than-429 as
  a hard error, so a legitimate "no rows" surfaced as
  `OpenF1FetchError` and aborted the past-race scan. Fixed in
  `openf1_client._fetch_with_retry` by matching the `No results found`
  body on 404 and returning `[]` for it; any other 404 body still
  raises. Pinned by a new `test_404_with_no_results_body_returns_empty_list`
  in `tests/test_openf1_client.py`.
- *Real `/v1/stints` carries rows with `lap_start` / `lap_end` set to
  `null`* for a driver whose stint never completed a flying lap (lap-1
  crash, DNS allocation row). `_race_stints` and
  `_historical_stint_rates` previously crashed on `int(None)`. Both
  now skip the row. No visible behaviour change in the derived
  outputs (a null-range stint has no laps to contribute anyway) — the
  change just stops the crash and lets the past-season scan continue.

**How ticket 23 should use this** (clear seam for the cross-source
merge):

Ticket 23 is expected to add a shared past-race aggregator that reads
`load_past_race_samples(...)` from `openf1_loader` for `season >= 2023`
and a parallel helper from `fastf1_loader` (currently
`fastf1_loader._pit_losses_in` + `_historical_stint_rates`, which take
a FastF1 `race` object) for `season < 2023`. The merger:

1. Loops `season in range(era_start(race_season), race_season)` over
   past seasons at the circuit (through `location_group(circuit)`).
2. For each season, picks the source: OpenF1 for `>= 2023`, FastF1
   otherwise. Collects `PastRaceSamples` either way (ticket 23 should
   wrap the FastF1 helpers in a parallel adapter so both sources
   produce the same dataclass).
3. Applies the recent-seasons weight `0.5 ** (race_season - season - 1)`
   and the same `lap_count × season_weight` weighted mean already in
   `openf1_loader._past_races_at_circuit` and
   `fastf1_loader._past_races_at_circuit`'s tails. Extract this tail
   into a shared helper so neither loader owns it.
4. `openf1_loader._past_races_at_circuit`'s current standalone
   behaviour (OpenF1-only range) stays in place as a fallback the
   target-weekend load uses directly; ticket 23 replaces its call
   site in `load_weekend` with the merged version. The dataclass
   `PastRaceSamples` and the public `load_past_race_samples` function
   are the seam that doesn't change.

**Deviations from the ticket / spec.**

- *`predict()` + `compare()` scoring is pinned to shape today, not to
  known 2024 scores.* The acceptance criterion "load and score through
  `predict()` and `compare()`" is met in the sense that a 2024 Monza
  weekend loaded through this loader produces a `RaceWeekend` the
  predictor consumes without raising. End-to-end scores for the three
  sampled weekends will match / explain their known 2024 scores only
  once ticket 23's router adds the 2022 FastF1 slice to a 2024 race's
  history (one 2023 OpenF1 past race alone is a smaller history budget
  than the two-season {2022, 2023} the FastF1 loader historically
  used), which is explicitly ticket 23's responsibility and ticket
  24's rerun verifies.

**Open questions, known limitations and follow-ups.**

- *A 2023 race's Pit Loss comes back 0.0 from this loader alone*, with
  `pit_loss_seasons=()`. The predictor's `simulator` treats Pit Loss as
  `pit_loss * (len(compounds) - 1)` in the Simulated Race Time, so a
  0.0 Pit Loss would silently make every multi-stop Candidate Strategy
  look cheaper than it should. Ticket 23's router must merge the 2022
  FastF1 slice before the 2023 race hits `predict()`; until then,
  never route a 2023 race through this loader on its own.
- *The missing-end-event cap and the Monza-2024 red-flag practice case
  from ticket 21 are not emitted anywhere the Backtest's Excluded Race
  logging sees them yet.* They are logged at WARNING on
  `openf1_loader._LOGGER` and returned as the second tuple from
  `_neutralisation_periods`, but `load_weekend` discards the warnings.
  Follow-up ticket to thread them into a quality-warning record on
  `RaceOutcome` / `BacktestResult` (recorded as a needs-triage ticket
  in the next session's record if the user wants to track it).
- *Historical Degradation stint_count stays tiny for a 2024 race going
  through this loader alone (one past season).* Ticket 23 fixes this
  by adding the 2022 FastF1 samples.
