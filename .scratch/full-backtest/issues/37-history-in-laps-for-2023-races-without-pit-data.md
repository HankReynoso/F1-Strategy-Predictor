# 37: Historical Degradation keeps in-laps for 2023 races without pit data

**What to build:** When OpenF1 has no `/v1/pit` rows for a past race (the first seven 2023 races, ticket 28), the OpenF1 past-race sampler rebuilds pit rows from Stint boundaries for Pit Loss, but still passes the empty `rows["pit"]` to the Historical Degradation fit. Each Stint's slow in-lap therefore stays in the fit and pushes Degradation up. This feeds the history for 2024 and 2025 races at Bahrain, Jeddah, Melbourne, Baku, Miami and Monaco. Use the same derived pit rows for both.

**Decisions and alternatives rejected.**

- *Pass the existing `pit_rows` variable instead of deriving the rows a second time inside `_historical_stint_rates`.* This is the smallest change, and it makes it impossible for the two samples to read different pit rows again inside this function.
  - Rejected: having `_historical_stint_rates` call `_pit_rows_from_stints` itself. That would put the empty-`/v1/pit` rule in two places, which is the same kind of drift this ticket fixes.
  - Rejected: moving the fallback into `_load_past_race_rows` so `rows["pit"]` is already filled in. That is tidier, but it changes what "the `/v1/pit` rows" means for every reader of the row dict. It belongs in ticket 45's history module.
- *Test through `load_past_race_samples`, not `_historical_stint_rates`.* The bug was in the wiring between the two samples, not in the fit. A unit test of the fit would have passed before the fix.
- *The before/after record uses a cache-only client and `git archive HEAD`.* It does not use `git stash`, because another session (ticket 39) is working in the same checkout.

**Deviations from the ticket / spec.**

- *Six races, not seven.* 2023 Emilia Romagna is a Cancelled Race and has given no samples since ticket 29, so it has no before/after. It is listed in the table for completeness.
- No spec change: `spec.md` does not describe which laps the fit drops.
- ADR-0006 corrected (criterion 4). The `/v1/pit` gap paragraph now says both history samples were affected. The ticket-28 paragraph now says both read the derived rows.
- `CONTEXT.md` gained **In-lap** (with **Out-lap**). The term was used in tickets and code comments but had no definition.

**Open questions, known limitations and follow-ups.**

- *Historical Degradation can rest on one or two short Stints and come out implausible.* Examples: 2024 Australian Soft is 2.53 s/lap from a single 7-lap 2023 Stint, and 2023 Miami Soft is now −3.48 s/lap from one 4-lap Stint. Many Medium rates at Baku, Jeddah and Melbourne are negative after fuel correction. `degradation.py` uses Historical Degradation whenever it exists, with no minimum Stint count. This ticket made a few of these values more extreme, because removing the In-lap leaves shorter Stints. New ticket 47 (`needs-triage`).
- *Monaco may be counted twice* (ticket 38). If so, the Monaco 2024/2025 rates above double-weight 2023 Monaco. That is independent of this fix.
- The baseline in `results/backtest/` is stale for 2024 Bahrain and 2024 Australian. Ticket 24's planned rerun after tickets 37–40 picks this up.
- The FastF1 copy of this logic reads real pit data, so it never had this bug. Ticket 45 removes the duplication.

**Review feedback to the user.**

- The fix is one argument, and the only effect is on races that read Historical Degradation from the six 2023 gap races. In this run, that changed scores at 2024 Bahrain (better) and 2024 Australian (slightly worse). Two races are too few to call it a net effect. The full rerun (ticket 24) is where to judge it.
- The ticket said Historical Degradation was "pushed up". That held for every Stint, but because so many rates are close to zero, several dropped below zero. Ticket 47 is the more important finding for prediction quality.
