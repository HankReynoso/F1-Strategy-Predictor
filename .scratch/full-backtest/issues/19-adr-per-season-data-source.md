## Implementation notes

**What was built.** Documentation only. [`docs/adr/0006-per-season-data-source.md`](../../../docs/adr/0006-per-season-data-source.md) records the per-season data-source policy with four sections: the decision itself (OpenF1 for 2023+, FastF1 for 2022 and earlier, behind the loader seam), the OpenF1 licence constraint, the 2018–2020 pre-era Pit Loss hole and why borrowing from a later season was rejected, and the mixed-source history case (one weekend's history spanning both sources, loaded per past season from its own source). Considered Options list the four alternatives the triage identified: wait for a FastF1 fix, F1 TV authentication (the reversible future option), OpenF1-only including 2022, and borrowing Pit Loss from a later same-circuit race. [`docs/adr/0004-history-within-regulation-era.md`](../../../docs/adr/0004-history-within-regulation-era.md) gains one paragraph on the Pit Loss exception noting that 2018–2020 is no longer downloadable, with a pointer to ADR-0006.

**Decisions and reasoning.**
- Numbered the ADR 0006, following the next-numbered convention the ticket calls for (existing ADRs are 0001–0005 under new-numbering, plus mirrored 01–08 under the old-numbering kept for issue tickets).
- Framed the licence constraint as "accepted now, revisit if the project's scope changes" rather than a hard rule, matching the ADR style of the other decisions in `docs/adr/` which record trade-offs, not prohibitions. The share-alike clause is called out because mixing an MIT source (FastF1) with a copyleft-adjacent source (OpenF1) narrows the project's effective licence to OpenF1's — a surprise worth stating in the ADR, not just a note.
- Explained the mixed-source history case explicitly even though the actual merging code is ticket 23's. The ADR records *why* the router loads each past season from its own source (future-leak avoidance, identical weekend-description shape across sources). An ADR that only said "the router dispatches per season" would hide the real trade-off.
- Named the five affected 2022 circuits (Australia, Canada, Belgium, Singapore, Japan) in the ADR rather than only in the ticket, so a future contributor hitting the pre-era Pit Loss code path can see which races the hole covers without reading the issue tracker.
- Did not add the FastF1 rate-limit history or the first-attempt baseline story to the ADR. Those belong in ticket 09's attempt log; the ADR links to it for the detail.

**Deviations from the ticket.** None material. The triage listed five decisions and three considered options; the ADR records all five decisions and four considered options (added "borrow Pit Loss from a later season" as its own rejected option because the triage note treats it as a real trade-off worth capturing).

**Open questions and limitations.**
- The ADR commits to the policy as it stands on 2026-10-23. If FastF1's unauthenticated path is restored by upstream, or OpenF1's coverage extends back, the trade-off shifts — reopen the ADR rather than silently changing the router.
- No new `CONTEXT.md` term was needed. "Weekend description", "loader seam" and "season router" are implementation words that don't rise to glossary status; the ADR uses existing terms (Pit Loss, Historical Degradation, Regulation Era, Stint, Tyre Set, Compound).
